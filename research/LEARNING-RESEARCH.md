# How to learn survival phrases in 3 weeks: evidence base, competitors, recommendations for the PWA

Date: 2026-11-18. Learner profile: adult Russian-speaking engineer, **near-beginner** (once studied a little Japanese, forgot almost all of it), 3 weeks × 20–30 min/day, then use during the trip (konbini, restaurants, photo/watch shops, a child). App: offline PWA on a Pixel (Chrome), EN interface, JA⇄EN, device TTS, Leitner.
Related documents: `PLAN.md`, `curriculum/CURRICULUM.md` (79 lessons, core of 20 lessons ≈250 units).

Evidence-strength labels: **[A]** meta-analysis/large studies; **[B]** individual controlled experiments; **[C]** expert consensus/practice, review blogs; **[?]** primary source not checked.

---

## 1. What works for a short horizon (3 weeks + the trip)

| Technique | Effect (size) | Strength | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spaced vs massed repetition** | L2 meta-analysis Kim & Webb 2022 (48 experiments, N=3411): g = 0.58 on the immediate test, **g = 0.80 on the delayed one**. Equal and expanding intervals are statistically equivalent. Long intervals lose to short ones immediately but win on delay. | [A] | Leitner 0/1/2/4/7 fits; no reason to "speed up" with complex algorithms (SM-2/FSRS) over 3 weeks. |
| **The optimal interval depends on the retention period** | Cepeda et al. 2006 (839 comparisons), 2008 (N>1350): the best gap grows with the retention interval; for retention of a few weeks the optimum is ≈ 10–20 % of it. | [A] | A phrase must be remembered up to 3–6 weeks ahead (to the end of the trip) → 2–7-day intervals are right. Before departure, a "run-through" of the whole core is needed. |
| **Testing effect (retrieval practice)** | Adesope et al. 2017 (272 effects): g ≈ 0.6 on average (0.51 vs rereading per summaries) [?exact figure — summaries differ, 0.61/0.70]; Rowland 2014: g = 0.50. The effect is larger when the practice format = the real-use format. | [A] | Every card: **first an attempt to recall, then the answer**. No "flipping through" without an attempt. |
| **Format match (transfer-appropriate processing)** | Webb 2009: productive learning of pairs yields productive knowledge better, receptive yields receptive; transfer between directions is partial. | [B] | Train "say" phrases EN→JA **aloud**; "hear" utterances **audio→meaning**. No production needed for hear. |
| **Production effect (saying aloud)** | Fawcett 2013: g ≈ 0.37 (between groups, recognition); within a list the effect is larger. | [A, but small effect] | A "said it aloud → show answer" button is cheap and useful. |
| **Interleaving** | Brunmair & Richter 2019 (59 studies): g = 0.42 on average, **but for words and texts — ambiguous/zero**; works best on similar visual categories. | [A] | Interleaving makes sense for **similar kana** (シ/ツ, ソ/ン, ぬ/め, ね/れ/わ). For phrases, mixing across categories is the default in review; don't build it specially. |
| **Chunks vs grammar first** | Meta-analysis: formulaic sequences are processed faster (small-to-medium advantage); there are **no** instructional meta-analyses of "learn in chunks" for adult beginners; a systematic review on children found no reliable data. | [B/C] | SLA consensus (Nation, Boers) and phrasebook practice: for survival — chunks + 10–13 slot patterns. The curriculum is already built this way. |
| **Listening to multiple voices (HVPT)** | Mahdi et al. 2024: g = 0.77, best with 5–8 talkers; Uchihara et al. 2025 (79 studies) — HVPT consistently helps perception. | [A for phonetics] | Different TTS voices (if the system has more than one) + speed 1.0/0.85. Native recordings would be better, but they are expensive. |
| **Shadowing** | No meta-analyses; there are small studies (Hamada 2016 — gain in listening comprehension) [?]. | [C] | A "repeat after the speaker" option = the same as "say aloud" after ▶. Don't build a separate mode. |
| **Pictures (dual coding)** | Carpenter & Olson 2012: pictures beat translation **only if** learners' overconfidence is removed; otherwise the effect is conditional. | [B] | Pictures for 1000 phrases are expensive content with doubtful payoff. Don't do it (category icons only). |
| **Kana mnemonics (Tofugu style)** | Matsunaga 2003: mnemonics beat flashcards on the **delayed** test; other studies (2009, NCOLCTL; CiNii) — no effect. | [B, mixed] | Cheap (one hint line per character) — give it as a hint after an error, not as a mandatory step. |
| **Romaji as a crutch** | Okuyama 2007 (CALICO): romaji did **not improve** word retention in beginners; the decisive factor was **intensive audio**. Japanese people read romaji slower than hiragana. | [B] | Hide romaji by default after week 1; audio always. See section 3. |
| **Sleep** | Mazza et al. 2016: with sleep between sessions, relearning is 2× faster (≈3 vs 6 passes); a week later 15 vs 11 of 16 words. Real-life Memrise data did not show a sleep effect. | [B] | Two short sessions per day, the second in the evening before bed. |
| **Gamification** | Lee & Baek 2023 (11 cases, Korea): g ≈ 0.52, in journal articles ≈ 0.29; for Duolingo mostly reviews rather than controlled studies. | [C] | The effect is weak and mostly motivational; a day streak and a progress bar are enough. |
| **Relearning (side note)** | Hansen et al. 2002 (304 people after Japan/Korea): forgotten vocabulary is relearned noticeably faster than new; de Bot et al. 2004 — the same for foreign words; Bahrick 1984 — part of the knowledge lasts for decades. | [A/B] | Our learner has few "remnants", but hiragana and basic phrases will come back faster. So a **short kana entry test** is needed, so as not to spend days on what will resurface in an hour. |
| **Pretest before a lesson** | Pan & Carpenter 2023 (review): attempting to answer before studying often improves retention — **if the correct answer is shown right afterwards**. | [A/B] | A lesson can open with "guess the meaning from audio" (2 min), then study. It also catches already-known units. |

**Session length and rhythm.** There are no direct meta-analyses of "minutes per day" for language apps [C]. From the evidence above: 2 short sessions are better than one long one (spacing + sleep); keep a session within 10–15 min — attention lasts, and it is easy to repeat.

## 2. Competitors: what to take, what to avoid

Prices are approximate, from reviews (often affiliate ones); check on the sites.

| Product | For our case | Take (one line) | Pricing model | Complaints |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Duolingo** (JA) | Weak for a trip: random vocabulary set, few situations | 3–5 min micro-lessons, day streak, instant feedback | Freemium, Super subscription | Kanji without system, no explanations, course often reworked (2023, 2026 — complaints) |
| **Busuu** | Medium | Short dialogues with a situation | Freemium/subscription | Little JA content, community checking is uneven |
| **Babbel** | **No** Japanese | — | — | — |
| **LingoDeer** | Good as a course, but not a phrasebook | Clear grammar notes for patterns, offline | Subscription ≈$15/mo, lifetime ≈$160 | Slow to reach tourist situations |
| **JapanesePod101** | Has "Survival Phrases" | Podcast format: dialogue → breakdown → repetition | Subscription, multi-tier | Aggressive upsells, long ads in episodes |
| **Pimsleur** | **Best for speaking by ear** | Audio question "how do you say…?" → pause → speaker's answer (recall + answer aloud with expanding intervals) | Subscription ≈$20/mo [?] | Little vocabulary, no reading, expensive, monotonous |
| **Anki** (+ Core 2k/6k, phrasebook decks) | Powerful, but you have to assemble it yourself | Flexible cards, audio on the card, "Again/Hard/Good" | Free (Android/desktop), iOS paid | High entry threshold, review "walls", no situational context |
| **Memrise** | Medium | Videos of native speakers saying the phrase (real speech) | Freemium | Shift towards AI chat, official courses cut back |
| **Renshuu** | Good free SRS | Fine-grained mode settings, many question types | Free + Pro ≈$7/mo | Cluttered interface |
| **Bunpro** | Not for 3 weeks (JLPT grammar) | Pattern as a card with substitution into a sentence | ≈$5/mo | Supplement only |
| **WaniKani** | Not for 3 weeks (kanji over years) | Mnemonic + strict SRS, a lesson unlocks after reviews | ≈$9/mo, 3 levels free | Slow, can't speed up |
| **Tofugu Learn Hiragana/Katakana + Kana Quiz** | **The benchmark for kana** | Picture mnemonic per character + speed quiz ("the whole row in 20 s") | Free | Unofficial claims of "in a few hours" are anecdotal |
| **Drops** | Weak | 5-minute timed sessions, large visual presentation | Freemium (5 min/day free) | Content errors, no phrases/grammar |
| **Mondly** | Weak | Situational chatbot dialogue | ≈$10/mo | Words repeated "in a vacuum", superficial |
| **Rocket Japanese** | Medium | Role-play dialogues with recording your own voice and comparing | One-off payment ≈$100–260 | No SRS, expensive |
| **Google Translate** (camera, "Conversation") | **The main tool during the trip** | — (delegate, don't copy) | Free | Offline conversation is limited (on Pixel 9+ offline Live Translate JA↔EN [?]) |
| **VoiceTra** (NICT, Japanese government) | Good voice translator for tourists | — | Free | Needs internet |

**Market conclusion:** nobody covers the "trip in 3 weeks" niche. Courses (Duolingo, LingoDeer) start from grammar and are slow to reach the konbini till. Phrasebooks don't train, and SRS (Anki) gives no situations. Two strengths of ours that competitors lack: **staff utterances with linked replies** and the **listening price trainer**. The main thing worth taking from others is the Pimsleur model "question → pause → answer aloud".

## 3. Japanese specifics

| Question | What is known | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Kana in a few days** | Hiragana 46 + katakana 46 — a realistic 4–8 h with mnemonics + a speed quiz [C]. Mnemonics: mixed data [B]. Similar characters are a classic error point (interleaving for similar categories works [A]). | Day 1 — a short hiragana entry test (something may resurface). Hiragana days 1–2, katakana days 3–5 (it matters more for photo/tech: カメラ, レンズ, ボディ). A "character → sound" speed quiz + a "similar pairs" block. Goal: read a katakana shop word in < 2–3 s. |
| **Romaji** | Experiment: does not help word retention, audio matters more [B]. Teachers' consensus: a "crutch" that hinders reading signs and pronunciation (r, ū, ō) [C]. But for a 3-week tourist starting from zero, dropping romaji entirely in the first days would slow the start [C]. | **Week 1:** romaji shown small. **From week 2:** hidden by default, revealed by tap; toggle in settings. Always keep romaji search. No romaji on the "show to a Japanese person" card. |
| **Furigana** | Standard in beginner textbooks [C]. | Furigana always above kanji in study modes; on "show" — without furigana (as in PLAN). |
| **Pitch accent** | Affects intelligibility (Schaefer & Darcy 2020), but for a tourist, in the context of a situation, short patterns are understood even with errors [C]. | Don't teach it. Repeating after TTS gives a "free" approximation. |
| **Keigo** | A tourist needs to **recognise** staff keigo (お召し上がりですか, ご利用ですか, お預かりします) and speak です/ます themselves [C]. | As in the curriculum: hear units for recognition only; don't require keigo production. |
| **Fast live speech** | Cashiers speak fast, contract, whisper "ませ". TTS is too clean and uniform [C]. Different voices help perception [A]. | Main mode — speed 1.0 (not 0.7); 0.7 only as a hint after an error. Before the trip, suggest the user watch 2–3 real "first-person konbini" recordings on YouTube (outside the app). |
| **Numbers and counters** | The 万 place and sound changes (さんびゃく, はっせん) are the main source of errors; prices are heard every day [C]. | A generated "price audio → type the digits" trainer with realistic prices (as in PLAN 4.8) — **2–3 min every day**, no skipping. Counters: つ, 人, 枚, 本 — enough. |

## 4. Recommendations for our app (by benefit/effort ratio)

| # | Recommendation | Benefit | Effort | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Attempt first, then the answer — everywhere.** Card: prompt (EN or audio) → "said it aloud / thought it" → answer + ▶ → 3 self-grading buttons. No "just flip through" mode. | High | Low | Testing effect g≈0.5–0.6 [A]; production effect [A] |
| 2 | **Mode by unit type:** say/pattern — EN→JA aloud (main) + JA→EN; hear — **only audio→meaning and audio→choose a reply**, no text before the answer. | High | Low | Transfer-appropriate processing [B]; Okuyama — audio matters more [B] |
| 3 | **Daily listening price trainer** (2–3 min, a mandatory block of the "Lesson of the day"), speed 1.0. | High | Medium (generator already in the plan) | Daily need during the trip; spacing [A] |
| 4 | **"Lesson of the day" = 2 sessions:** morning 12–15 min (reviews → 1 new lesson of 10–15 units → quiz), evening 8–10 min before bed (review today's new items + prices). If reviews > 60, give no new items (as in CURRICULUM). | High | Low | Spacing [A], sleep [B] |
| 5 | **Dialogue chains "heard it → reply within 3 s"**: a staff utterance plays, a timer runs, choose/say a reply from `replies`, then the next utterance. The `replyTo/replies` data already exists. | High | Medium | Format = real use [A/B]; Pimsleur model |
| 6 | **Kana: entry test + speed quiz + similar-character pairs.** Rows read correctly and fast (< 2 s) are skipped. On error show the mnemonic (a line of text, no pictures). Final goal — reading katakana words from shops. | High (week 1) | Medium | Interleaving of similar items [A]; mnemonics [B, mixed]; savings [B] |
| 7 | **Romaji: visible in week 1, then hidden by default** (tap to show). Furigana — always in study modes. | Medium | Low | Okuyama 2007 [B]; consensus [C] |
| 8 | **Lesson pretest (1–2 min):** "what does this mean?" from audio for 5 units of the new lesson, then the correct answer right away. Units answered correctly go straight to box 2. | Medium | Low | Pretesting [A/B] |
| 9 | **Pre-flight run-through (days −3…0):** all core units — one "audio→meaning" pass + prices; errors go to "Trip favourites". | Medium | Low | Spacing/ridgeline [A] |
| 10 | **Trip mode "before a situation":** choose a situation (till, camera shop, taxi) → a 3-minute run-through of its hear+say units. A "came in handy / didn't understand" diary → not-understood utterances go into the SRS. | Medium | Low | Retrieval immediately before use [C] |
| 11 | **Voices and speed:** if the system has several ja-JP voices — alternate; otherwise speed 1.0 + occasional 0.85. Keep the `audio` field for future native recordings. | Medium | Low | HVPT [A] (TTS is a weak substitute) |
| 12 | **Motivation — the minimum:** day streak, core progress "N/250", a "ready for the konbini till" badge after completing the dialogue. No points, leagues, hearts. | Low–medium | Low | Gamification g≈0.3–0.5, [C] |
| 13 | **Reminders — not via the PWA.** Offline local notifications in Chrome Android are unreliable (Notification Triggers never shipped, Periodic Background Sync is imprecise and depends on engagement; push needs a server). Instead: a "Set 2 alarms/reminders in Calendar" screen with ready text and a link to the PWA. | Medium | Very low | Chrome documentation [A for the tech] |
| 14 | **Lesson unlocking by SRS (≥80 % in box ≥2) — keep it, but with a "Skip test" button** (8 of 10 correct → lesson counted). Otherwise a slower learner gets stuck and a fast one gets bored. | Medium | Low | Pretesting/savings [B] |
| 15 | **What NOT to build:** speech recognition with pronunciation scoring (network, unreliable for JA); pitch training; Japanese keyboard input and kanji writing; pictures for every phrase; AI chat; FSRS/SM-2; leagues and social features; a built-in translator (delegate to Google Translate). | — | Saves 10+ h | Sections 1–3 |

## 5. Limitations of this review
- Most meta-analyses concern English as an L2 and school/university samples; transfer to an adult "from zero in 3 weeks" is an extrapolation.
- Competitor prices and course status come from reviews, many of them affiliate or written by competitors (Migaku, LingoDeer, Tofugu); verify.
- The exact g in Adesope 2017 and the percentages in Cepeda 2008 differ across summaries; primary sources were not opened (paywall/403).
- Expertise reversal (Kalyuga et al. 2003): supports useful to a beginner hinder a more advanced learner — the basis for hiding romaji after week 1 [from memory, not verified by search].
